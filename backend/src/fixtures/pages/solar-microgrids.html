Solar microgrids light up rural Karnataka

Across the dry districts of northern Karnataka, village-scale solar microgrids are changing how families, farmers and small shops use electricity. A typical installation pairs 30 to 50 kilowatts of rooftop and ground-mounted panels with a lithium battery bank, serving between 80 and 200 households.

Operators report that reliable evening power has extended study hours for children and allowed tailoring units and flour mills to run after sunset. Irrigation pumps on dedicated feeders run during daylight, easing pressure on the state grid during peak hours.

Challenges remain. Battery replacement costs, tariff collection and competition from subsidised grid connections can strain finances. Analysts say pay-as-you-go metering and community ownership models improve repayment rates. With falling panel prices, planners expect hybrid grid-connected microgrids to become the norm, letting villages sell surplus daytime power back to the distribution company.
